Commercial Epoxy Flooring in Spokane Valley, WA

There is no such thing as a commercial floor system. There is a warehouse system, a kitchen system, a shop system and a clinic system, and specifying the wrong one is the most expensive mistake in this trade — you find out in year two, with product on the racks and no way to redo it cheaply. This page is organized the way the decision actually gets made: by what happens in the building, then by how we get it done without closing you.

Pick the System by What Happens in the Room

Warehouse and distribution. The load is wheels and point pressure, not chemicals. A high-build epoxy body with a urethane or polyaspartic wear layer handles it, and the real money is in joint treatment — unfilled control joints are what forklift wheels chip into potholes. Semi-rigid filler in every joint before coating.

Automotive and fabrication. Oil, brake fluid, battery acid, dropped steel, hot tires. 100% solids epoxy with a chemically resistant topcoat, and aggregate in the bay aisles. Skip the high gloss; it shows every drip and gets slick under coolant.

Food service and beverage production. Urethane mortar, not epoxy. Hot water and caustic sanitizers soften standard epoxy, and thermal shock from a steam line delaminates it. Integral coved base is not optional here — it removes the floor-to-wall seam that health inspections focus on.

Medical, dental and clinical. Seamless, light-colored, easy to disinfect, with coved base and no aggregate that traps soil. Static-dissipative options where equipment demands it.

Retail and showroom. Appearance-led. Flake, quartz or polished concrete, depending on whether you want color control or the lowest lifetime maintenance.

The Joints Are the Job

Owners look at the field of the floor. Failures start at the edges.

Control joints in a warehouse slab spall from the inside out. A hard wheel hits the unsupported edge of a saw cut a thousand times a day, chips it, and the chip widens until it is a trench that damages equipment and collects debris. Filling those joints with a semi-rigid material before coating supports both edges and is the single highest-value line item on most industrial quotes.

The wall line matters too. In any wash-down environment, the ninety-degree corner where slab meets wall is where water and organics sit. A coved base carries the coating up the wall four to six inches and removes the corner entirely.

Loading dock edges, trench drains and equipment pads all get detailed by hand. When one bid is dramatically cheaper than another on the same building, this detail work is usually what got left out.

Working Around Your Operating Hours

Almost every commercial job in Spokane Valley is done in phases, because almost nobody can go dark for a week.

We section the building with poly containment, run dust-collected grinders so nothing drifts onto product or into a clean area, and coat one zone at a time. Material choice follows the schedule rather than the other way around: where the space has to be back in service fast, a polyurea or polyaspartic build gets foot traffic in hours and wheels in about a day, versus several days for a conventional epoxy cure.

Night and weekend work carries a premium. It is almost always smaller than the cost of the downtime it replaces, and we will show you both numbers so it is your call rather than ours.

One planning note specific to this region: winter installs in unheated warehouse space need the slab held above curing temperature, which means staged heaters and a longer schedule. Booking that work for shoulder season saves real money if your calendar allows it.

Budgeting a Commercial Floor

Expect $4 to $12 per square foot installed, and the spread is almost entirely system choice.

$4 to $6 — straightforward warehouse build over a sound slab, minimal joint work.

$6 to $9 — full flake or quartz broadcast, aggregate for traction, safety striping, moderate joint treatment.

$9 to $12+ — urethane mortar, coved base, trench drain detailing, clinical or food-grade requirements.

On top of the system: surface preparation runs $1.50 to $3.50 per square foot when there is an existing coating or adhesive to remove, moisture mitigation $1.00 to $1.75 where testing calls for it, and off-hours phasing as a stated premium. Slab repair is quoted from the walkthrough, not estimated from a photo.

Every proposal we send names the system, the total mil thickness, the joint treatment, and the zones getting aggregate. If a competing bid does not name those four things, the two quotes are not describing the same floor.

Coating or Polishing?

For a sound slab in a warehouse with no chemical exposure, polished concrete usually wins on lifetime cost — nothing bonded on top means nothing to delaminate, and no recoat cycle.

A coating wins whenever you need chemical resistance, a specific color, safety striping, a seamless wash-down surface, or a slab too damaged to polish evenly. Those requirements are common enough that most of our commercial work is still coating work.

We quote both when both are viable and tell you which one we would buy for our own building. If a contractor only sells one of the two, you are getting a product recommendation rather than advice.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many days will my business be shut down?

Usually none. We phase the building into sections, work nights or weekends, and hand back each area once it is walkable. On a fast-cure polyurea build a section coated Friday evening takes racking again Sunday. Full closure only makes sense when the whole floor has to move at once.

Which system does a commercial kitchen need?

Urethane mortar, not standard epoxy. Kitchens combine hot water, animal fats and daily caustic cleaning, which softens epoxy over time. Urethane mortar handles thermal shock and gets installed with an integral coved base so there is no seam where the floor meets the wall for debris to sit in.

Can a coating meet OSHA slip requirements?

Yes, by specifying it. Aggregate is broadcast into the topcoat at a density matched to the area, so wet zones get a coarser profile than dry aisles. Ask any bidder to state the additive and the zones on the quote rather than accepting the words slip resistant on their own.

What does commercial epoxy cost per square foot?

Roughly $4 to $12 in the Spokane Valley market depending on the system. A basic warehouse build sits at the bottom, a full flake or quartz build in the middle, and urethane mortar with coved base at the top. Surface preparation and off-hours scheduling are separate lines.
